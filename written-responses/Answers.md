Question 1.Is there any situation that describes an unconventional route taken in your experience?
If so, please share a paragraph or two about this.

Answer -
In one of my projects, I faced a challenge where the team was struggling to migrate
a DB2 database into Azure SQL.
The conventional approach was to rely on automated migration tools, but these repeatedly
failed due to schema incompatibilities and performance bottlenecks.
Instead of continuing down the same path, I took an unconventional route by manually
analyzing the most critical tables and queries, then writing custom scripts to handle
schema translation and optimize indexing strategies.
This approach was time‑consuming at first, but it allowed me to deeply understand the
differences between DB2 and Azure SQL. By focusing on the most business‑critical areas
first, I ensured that the migration could proceed without disrupting dependent
microservices.
Ultimately, this unconventional route not only solved the immediate problem but also
gave me insights into database optimization that I wouldn’t have gained through a
purely automated process.
It reinforced the value of thinking creatively and not being afraid to step outside
standard methods when the situation demands it.


Question 2. Do you recall a situation where you intensely and relentlessly focused to complete a task
(can be professional work or hobby) that helped you grow?
If so, please describe this experience in a paragraph or two.

Answer - For one of my projects, I had to study monolithic architecture in depth. 
Since the codebase was large and tightly coupled, I integrated GitHub Copilot directly 
into my IDE to accelerate my learning. I used Copilot to generate code snippets, explain
legacy functions, and suggest refactoring approaches, which helped me quickly understand how 
the presentation, business logic, and database layers interacted in a single deployable unit. 
By iteratively prompting Copilot, I could visualize bottlenecks like scaling issues and deployment
risks, and this hands-on exploration gave me a solid foundation to later compare 
monolithic systems with microservices.



Question 3. Describe an experience of joining new team, project or environment where there was significant amount of context, information or complexity to absorb in a relatively short period of time.
How did you approach learning, building clarity, prioritizing what to focus on and gradually becoming effective?

Answer - When I joined my previous project, I had to quickly understand an existing application, 
its business workflows, technical architecture, and the responsibilities of different modules.
The project involved legacy components, database dependencies, batch processes, and several 
integrations, which made it challenging to understand the complete system in a short period of 
time. Instead of trying to learn everything at once, I started by understanding the overall 
business purpose and identifying the modules most closely related to my assigned tasks. 
I reviewed existing documentation, studied the code flow, discussed my questions with experienced
team members, and used debugging and log analysis to understand how the application behaved in 
real scenarios.

I divided my learning into smaller, manageable areas and prioritized tasks based on their b
usiness impact and urgency. Initially, I focused on making small changes and resolving clearly
defined issues so that I could become familiar with the codebase while contributing to
the project. Gradually, I began handling more complex tasks independently and developed a better
understanding of the system's dependencies and potential risks. 
This experience taught me that becoming effective in a new environment requires curiosity, 
structured learning, clear communication, and consistent effort. 
It also helped me become more comfortable working with unfamiliar systems and adapting to
complex project requirements.
